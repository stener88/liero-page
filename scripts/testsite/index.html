Rope physics considered harmful: a field report

By A. Worm · October 1, 2026 · 6 min read

In 1998 a Finnish student released a small game in which two worms tear a cave apart with rockets, grenades and a rope that should not physically work. Twenty-eight years later, the rope still does not physically work, and nobody has complained.

Every page on the web is, structurally, a cave. Headlines are ledges. Paragraphs are layered sediment. Buttons are loose rocks waiting to be dislodged. Our reporter spent a week swinging between sidebars to find out what happens when you take that literally.

What we learned

First, footers make excellent floors. Second, a well-placed grenade turns a navigation bar into a staircase. Third, nobody reads the terms and conditions, but everybody stands on them.

Long paragraphs are the best terrain: dense, wide and easy to tunnel through. Short paragraphs leave gaps you can fall into. Bulleted lists, it turns out, are stairs.

  • Headlines: high ground
  • Images: solid cover
  • Whitespace: fall damage (none, actually)

We close with a warning: once you have stood on a paragraph, you will never read one the same way again.
